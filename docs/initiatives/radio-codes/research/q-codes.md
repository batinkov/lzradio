# Q-codes

Three-letter codes beginning with **Q**. The defining feature: each Q-code has
**two forms** — a question (interrogative) and an answer (affirmative). In CW the
question form was sent with a Morse `?` after it.

Checked against the ITU text (Rec. ITU-R M.1172) on 2026-10-13 — see
[Verification notes](#verification-notes).

## History (for the 2–3 sentence intro)

Q-codes began as a British government list of abbreviations for ships and coast
stations **c.1909**. Forty-five Q-codes were formalized in the "List of
Abbreviations" of the **Second International Radiotelegraph Convention (London, 1912)**, effective 1913. The modern set was standardized at the **ITU convention
in Atlantic City, 1947**, and most 1947 definitions are still current in the ITU
Radio Regulations.

## Structure

The full ITU allocation splits Q-codes into three ranges:

| Range       | Service             | The ham range? |
| ----------- | ------------------- | -------------- |
| **QAA–QNZ** | Aeronautical (ICAO) | No             |
| **QOA–QQZ** | Maritime Mobile     | No             |
| **QRA–QUZ** | All services        | **Yes**        |

The full list runs to "over a hundred" codes; the amateur working set is the
**~30 codes below** (all in QRA–QUZ).

How the two forms work, per the ITU text:

- A code is a **question** when followed by a question mark (`QRL?`).
- A code can be given an explicit affirmative or negative sense by adding `C`
  (yes) or `NO` after it.
- Blanks (`____`) are filled with call signs, frequencies, times or figures.

## Two layers of meaning

Each code has an **official ITU meaning** and, often, a looser **on-air ham
usage** that has drifted from it (`QRT` officially means "stop sending"; hams
use it for "closing down"). These are kept in separate columns on purpose — the
original notes mixed them in one cell.

## Ham Q-codes — both forms

Meanings paraphrased; treat as our own wording, cite ITU as source.

| Code | Question form (ITU)                                                  | Answer / statement form (ITU)                                   | On-air ham usage                                                                                          | Status      |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| QRA  | What is the name of your station?                                    | The name of my station is ____                                  | Often used for "call sign"; the ITU text says name only                                                   | less common |
| QRB  | How far are you from my station?                                     | The distance between our stations is about ____                 |                                                                                                           | less common |
| QRG  | What is my exact frequency?                                          | Your exact frequency is ____                                    |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRH  | Does my frequency vary?                                              | Your frequency varies                                           |                                                                                                           | less common |
| QRI  | How is the tone of my transmission?                                  | The tone of your transmission is ____ (scale 1–3)               |                                                                                                           | less common |
| QRK  | How readable are my signals?                                         | The readability of your signals is ____ (scale 1–5)             |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRL  | Are you busy?                                                        | I am busy, please do not interfere                              | `QRL?` = "is this frequency in use?", asked before calling CQ                                             | common      |
| QRM  | Is my transmission being interfered with?                            | Your transmission is being interfered with ____ (scale 1–5)     | Interference from other stations (man-made); hams usually say it about their own reception ("I have QRM") | common      |
| QRN  | Are you troubled by static?                                          | I am troubled by static ____ (scale 1–5)                        | Atmospheric noise                                                                                         | common      |
| QRO  | Shall I increase transmitter power?                                  | Increase transmitter power                                      |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRP  | Shall I decrease transmitter power?                                  | Decrease transmitter power                                      | "QRP" = low-power operating                                                                               | common      |
| QRQ  | Shall I send faster?                                                 | Send faster (____ wpm)                                          |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRS  | Shall I send more slowly?                                            | Send more slowly (____ wpm)                                     |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRT  | Shall I stop sending?                                                | Stop sending                                                    | "Going QRT" = closing down the station (the official closing signal is `CL`)                              | common      |
| QRU  | Have you anything for me?                                            | I have nothing for you                                          |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRV  | Are you ready?                                                       | I am ready                                                      |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QRX  | When will you call me again?                                         | I will call you again at ____                                   | "QRX" = wait, stand by                                                                                    | common      |
| QRZ  | Who is calling me?                                                   | You are being called by ____                                    | No other meaning — it is not a substitute for CQ                                                          | common      |
| QSA  | What is the strength of my signals?                                  | The strength of your signals is ____ (scale 1–5)                |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QSB  | Are my signals fading?                                               | Your signals are fading                                         |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QSD  | Are my signals mutilated?                                            | Your signals are mutilated                                      | Older lists word it as "Is my keying defective?"                                                          | rare        |
| QSK  | Can you hear me between your signals, and may I break in?            | I can hear you between my signals; break in                     | "QSK" = full break-in keying                                                                              | common      |
| QSL  | Can you acknowledge receipt?                                         | I am acknowledging receipt                                      | "QSL" = confirmed; "QSL card"                                                                             | common      |
| QSO  | Can you communicate with ____ directly (or by relay)?                | I can communicate with ____ directly (or by relay through ____) | "A QSO" = a contact                                                                                       | common      |
| QSP  | Will you relay to ____?                                              | I will relay to ____                                            |                                                                                                           | less common |
| QSX  | Will you listen to ____ on ____ kHz?                                 | I am listening to ____ on ____ kHz                              | Used when working split ("QSX 1822")                                                                      | common      |
| QSY  | Shall I change to another frequency?                                 | Change to another frequency (or to ____ kHz)                    | "QSY to …" = move frequency                                                                               | common      |
| QSZ  | Shall I send each word more than once?                               | Send each word twice (or ____ times)                            |                                                                                                           | less common |
| QTC  | How many messages have you to send?                                  | I have ____ messages for you                                    |                                                                                                           | common      |
| QTH  | What is your position (latitude and longitude, or other indication)? | My position is ____                                             | "My QTH" = my location                                                                                    | common      |
| QTR  | What is the correct time?                                            | The correct time is ____                                        |                                                                                                           | common      |

## Rating scales carried by Q-codes

Five codes take a numbered answer. Values per the ITU text.

| Code               | 1                    | 2        | 3           | 4        | 5         |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | -------- | ----------- | -------- | --------- |
| QRK (readability)  | bad                  | poor     | fair        | good     | excellent |
| QSA (strength)     | scarcely perceptible | weak     | fairly good | good     | very good |
| QRM (interference) | nil                  | slightly | moderately  | severely | extremely |
| QRN (static)       | nil                  | slightly | moderately  | severely | extremely |
| QRI (tone)         | good                 | variable | bad         | —        | —         |

## Amateur-only Q-codes (not in the ITU list)

- **QST** — general call to all amateurs ("CQ ARRL"). ARRL usage.

### Slang / informal (mark clearly as slang)

Ham humor and jargon. Not found in any primary source checked.

- **QLF** — "Try sending with your left foot" (jab at poor CW).
- **QRRR** — old ARRL land-distress signal (historical; SOS is the real one).
  Not shipped: no primary source found.

## Verification notes

Checked 2026-10-13 against ITU-R M.1172, the ARRL Q-signals sheet and the
ON4UN/ON4WW operating manual.

- **QSD** was listed here as slang. It is an official ITU code, though absent
  from both ham lists — hence "rare".
- **QRM** was worded from the receiving station's side ("Are you being
  interfered with?"). ITU and ARRL word it about the _sender's_ transmission.
  The ham reading is real (ON4UN uses it) and is now in the usage column.
  "Man-made" is in neither ITU nor ARRL.
- **QRT** — "closing down" is ham usage, not ITU.
- **QRA** — "call" is not in the ITU text.
- **QSX, QRB, QRI** were missing. QSX is in all three sources.
- **QST** added as amateur-only.
- All other entries match the ITU wording in substance.

**Open:** whether the rare/less-common codes (QRB, QRH, QRI, QSD, QSP, QSZ) and
the slang entries ship in v1.

# Q-codes

Three-letter codes beginning with **Q**. The defining feature: each Q-code has
**two forms** — a question (interrogative) and an answer (affirmative). In CW the
question form was sent with a Morse `?` after it.

## History (for the 2–3 sentence intro)

Q-codes began as a British government list of abbreviations for ships and coast
stations **c.1909**. Forty-five Q-codes were formalized in the "List of
Abbreviations" of the **Second International Radiotelegraph Convention (London,
1912)**, effective 1913. The modern set was standardized at the **ITU convention
in Atlantic City, 1947**, and most 1947 definitions are still current in the ITU
Radio Regulations.

## Structure

The full ITU allocation splits Q-codes into three ranges:

| Range | Service | The ham range? |
|---|---|---|
| **QAA–QNZ** | Aeronautical (ICAO) | No |
| **QOA–QQZ** | Maritime Mobile | No |
| **QRA–QUZ** | All services | **Yes** |

The full list runs to "over a hundred" codes; the amateur working set is the
**~25–40 codes below** (all in QRA–QUZ).

## Ham Q-codes — both forms

Meanings paraphrased; treat as our own wording, cite ITU as source.

| Code | Question form | Answer / statement form | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| QRA | What is your station's name/call? | My station is ____ | common |
| QRG | What is my exact frequency? | Your exact frequency is ____ | common |
| QRH | Does my frequency vary? | Your frequency varies | less common |
| QRK | What is the readability of my signal? | Your readability is ____ (1–5) | common |
| QRL | Are you busy? | I am busy, please do not interfere | common |
| QRM | Are you being interfered with? | I am being interfered with (man-made) | common |
| QRN | Are you troubled by static? | I am troubled by static (atmospheric) | common |
| QRO | Shall I increase power? | Increase power | common |
| QRP | Shall I decrease power? | Decrease power (→ "QRP" = low-power operating) | common |
| QRQ | Shall I send faster? | Send faster (____ wpm) | common |
| QRS | Shall I send more slowly? | Send more slowly (____ wpm) | common |
| QRT | Shall I stop sending? | Stop sending / I am closing down | common |
| QRU | Have you anything for me? | I have nothing for you | common |
| QRV | Are you ready? | I am ready | common |
| QRX | When will you call again? | I will call you again at ____ | common |
| QRZ | Who is calling me? | You are being called by ____ | common |
| QSA | What is the strength of my signal? | Your signal strength is ____ (1–5) | common |
| QSB | Are my signals fading? | Your signals are fading | common |
| QSK | Can you hear me between your signals? | I can hear you between my signals (full break-in) | common |
| QSL | Can you acknowledge receipt? | I acknowledge receipt (→ "QSL card") | common |
| QSO | Can you communicate with ____ directly? | I can communicate with ____ (→ "a QSO" = a contact) | common |
| QSP | Will you relay to ____? | I will relay to ____ | less common |
| QSY | Shall I change frequency? | Change to frequency ____ | common |
| QSZ | Shall I send each word more than once? | Send each word twice | less common |
| QTC | How many messages have you to send? | I have ____ messages for you | common |
| QTH | What is your position/location? | My location is ____ | common |
| QTR | What is the correct time? | The correct time is ____ | common |

### Amateur slang / informal Q-codes (mark clearly as slang)

Not ITU-official — ham humor and jargon.

- **QLF** — "Try sending with your left foot" (jab at poor CW).
- **QSD** — "Your keying is defective."
- **QRRR** — old ARRL land-distress signal (historical; SOS is the real one).

# Number codes

Numeric shorthand (73, 88, …). **Not Q-codes** — different origin, and **no
interrogative form**. Single fixed meaning each.

## History (for the intro)

These come from **landline telegraphy**, not radio. Western Union standardized a
**"92 code"** in **1859** (numbers 1–92 with fixed meanings), later absorbed into
the **Phillips Code**, a press-telegraphy shorthand published by **Walter P.
Phillips in 1879**. Radio inherited them wholesale. `73` reportedly meant "my love
to you" as early as 1857, shifting to "my compliments / best regards" in the 92
code.

## The codes actually used in amateur radio

| Code | Meaning | Notes | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **73** | Best regards | The universal sign-off. Singular — "73," never "73s." | common |
| **88** | Love and kisses | Warm sign-off; often between spouses / close friends. | common |
| **33** | Fondest regards | Traditionally **YL-to-YL** (between female operators); "love sealed with friendship." From the Young Ladies Radio League. | occasional |
| **55** | Best success / good luck | Mostly **European CW** (German "viel Erfolg"). Less standardized. | occasional |
| **161** | 73 + 88 | Combination (88+73=161); "best regards and love," used between couples. | niche |
| **30** | End / no more | Journalism/telegraph end-mark ("that's all"). Rare on air today but recognized. | archaic |
